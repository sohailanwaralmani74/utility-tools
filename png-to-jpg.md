  <h2>Why Convert PNG to JPG?</h2>
  <p>
    PNG is lossless and supports transparency, making it great for logos and icons. 
    JPG (or JPEG) is lossy but far more efficient for photographs and large visuals. 
    By converting <em>PNG to JPG</em>, you can often reduce file size, improve website 
    loading speed, and make your images compatible with more platforms.
  </p>

  <h3>When PNG is Better</h3>
  <p>
    Keep PNG for designs that need transparent backgrounds, screenshots with text, 
    or images where crisp edges matter most. In these cases, JPG conversion might 
    increase file size or blur details.
  </p>

  <h3>When JPG is Better</h3>
  <p>
    Use JPG for photos, digital art with gradients, or any large images where 
    file size matters. Converting from PNG to JPG can reduce storage needs and 
    speed up web delivery.
  </p>

  </p>

  <h2>FAQs on PNG to JPG Conversion</h2>

  <h3>Does converting PNG to JPG reduce quality?</h3>
  <p>
    Yes, JPG is a lossy format. Photographs usually look fine, 
    but images with text or flat colors may lose sharpness.
  </p>

  <h3>Why did my JPG get bigger after conversion?</h3>
  <p>
    If your PNG had transparency or very few colors, the PNG was 
    already highly optimized. Filling transparency with a solid color 
    and saving as JPG can increase file size.
  </p>

  <h3>Are JPG and JPEG the same?</h3>
  <p>
    Yes, JPG and JPEG are the same format. The different extensions 
    exist for historical reasons, but both work interchangeably.
